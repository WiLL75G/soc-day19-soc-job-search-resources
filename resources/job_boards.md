# Cybersecurity Job Boards and SOC Job Search Strategy

A practical reference for finding SOC and cybersecurity roles without relying on one job board or one application method.

The goal is not to apply everywhere.

The goal is to search consistently, find relevant roles early, tailor applications, and track the process.

---

## Job Boards

### Tier 1, General Job Platforms

| Platform | Why Use It |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn Jobs | Large professional network with direct access to recruiters, hiring managers, and company pages |
| Indeed | High volume of technology and cybersecurity listings |
| CyberSecJobs | Cybersecurity focused job listings |
| Dice | Technology focused job board with security roles |

These platforms are useful starting points because they provide broad coverage.

The limitation is competition.

A complete search strategy should include specialist platforms and professional communities as well.

---

### Tier 2, Cybersecurity Specific Platforms

| Platform | Why Use It |
|---|---|
| InfoSec Jobs | Information security focused listings with remote filtering |
| SANS Jobs | Security focused opportunities connected to the SANS community |
| ISC2 Career Resources | Cybersecurity career and employment resources |
| Cybersecurity Ventures Jobs | Industry specific cybersecurity listings |

Specialist platforms can surface roles that are harder to find through broad searches.

Availability and activity can change, so each platform should be checked periodically rather than assumed to remain equally useful.

---

### Tier 3, Networking and Community Channels

Traditional job boards are not the only place opportunities appear.

Useful additional channels include:

| Platform | How to Use It |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Follow SOC leaders, recruiters, MSSPs, and security teams |
| X | Follow cybersecurity recruiters, companies, and practitioners |
| Discord | Participate in legitimate blue team and cybersecurity communities |
| Reddit | Monitor cybersecurity career and employment discussions |
| GitHub | Maintain public evidence of technical work and contribute where appropriate |

These channels should complement conventional applications rather than replace them.

Professional relationships can expose opportunities, referrals, hiring discussions, and information that may not appear through a normal job board search. This is also the tier with the least competition, mainly because most applicants never look past the first two.

---

## Platforms With Geographic Restrictions

Some job sources may be useful only to applicants who meet specific citizenship, residency, or clearance requirements.

Examples include:

| Platform | Consideration |
|---|---|
| USAJobs | Many cybersecurity positions have US citizenship or other eligibility requirements |
| ClearanceJobs | Roles commonly require an existing or obtainable US security clearance |

Always check the individual vacancy requirements rather than assuming eligibility from the job title alone.

For an international remote search, time is better spent prioritizing roles where location and employment eligibility actually match.

## Search Terms

Do not search only for `SOC Analyst`.

Organizations use different titles for similar work.

Useful searches include:

```text
SOC Analyst Tier 1
Security Operations Center Analyst
Junior Cybersecurity Analyst
Junior Security Analyst
Information Security Analyst
SIEM Analyst
Security Monitoring Analyst
Blue Team Analyst
Threat Detection Analyst
Detection Engineer
Cyber Defense Analyst
Incident Response Analyst
```

Some titles represent more advanced roles depending on the employer.

Read the responsibilities rather than assuming seniority from the title alone.

## Remote Search Strategy

For remote work, combine the role with the target region.

Examples:

```text
SOC Analyst remote UK
SOC Analyst remote Europe
SOC Analyst remote UAE
SOC Analyst remote Australia
Security Analyst remote UK
Threat Detection Analyst remote Europe
```

Remote does not automatically mean worldwide.

A remote vacancy may still require residence in a particular country, employment eligibility, specific working hours, or timezone overlap.

Check those requirements before investing time in the application.

## Job Alerts

### LinkedIn

Create several alerts rather than one broad alert.

Examples:

```text
SOC Analyst
Security Analyst
Security Monitoring Analyst
Threat Detection Analyst
Detection Engineer
```

Use relevant remote and geographic filters.

### Indeed

Create alerts for the same core titles and adjust the search when irrelevant roles begin dominating the results.

### Company Career Pages

For companies that consistently hire security analysts, monitor the company's own careers page.

A company career page can also provide information that is missing or outdated on an aggregator.

### Search Engines

Search engines can help locate roles outside normal job boards.

Examples:

```text
"SOC Analyst" "remote" "hiring"
"Security Analyst" "remote" "careers"
"Tier 1 SOC Analyst" "remote"
```

Always verify the vacancy on the employer's official careers page before applying.

## Application Strategy

### 1. Apply While the Role Is Active

Applying reasonably early can reduce the chance of spending time on a vacancy that is already deep into its hiring process.

There is no universal number of hours or days that guarantees better consideration.

The useful principle is simpler: do not unnecessarily delay a strong application.

### 2. Tailor the Application

Read the job description before submitting. Identify the required skills, preferred skills, SIEM technology, endpoint technology, operating systems, networking requirements, incident response responsibilities, detection responsibilities, and certification requirements.

Then make sure relevant genuine experience is easy to find on the CV and portfolio.

Do not add skills simply because they appear in the job description. Only mirror terminology when it accurately describes work actually completed.

### 3. Connect the Portfolio to the Role

A portfolio should make technical claims easier to verify.

If a vacancy asks for Splunk experience, link the most relevant Splunk projects. If it asks for Windows investigation, prioritize Windows evidence. If it asks for network analysis, surface Wireshark work. If it asks for detection engineering, point toward detection rules and validation projects.

The objective is not "here are 28 repositories." The objective is "here is the evidence most relevant to this role."

### 4. Follow Up When Appropriate

A professional follow up can be useful when contact information is available and the employer's process allows it. Keep it short. The purpose is not to pressure the recruiter, it is to make the connection between the application and relevant experience clear.

### 5. Track Every Application

Maintain a simple application tracker with company, role, country, remote requirements, date found, date applied, job URL, contact, application status, follow up date, interview stage, and notes.

Tracking prevents duplicate applications and makes it easier to understand which search channels and application approaches are producing responses.

## Companies Worth Monitoring

### MSSPs, a strong entry point

MSSPs are the strongest entry point for an entry level SOC search. Their business model depends on hiring Tier 1 analysts at volume to support multiple customer environments, and they are generally more willing to take a candidate with lab work and no formal experience than an in house SOC will be.

Examples worth checking:

- Secureworks
- Arctic Wolf
- Trustwave
- Optiv
- Herjavec Group

Do not assume every MSSP hires entry level analysts or accepts candidates from every location. Evaluate each vacancy individually, and check current openings through official careers pages since this list will drift over time.

### Cleared and government contractors

Examples include Leidos, Booz Allen Hamilton, SAIC, and ManTech. Almost all of these require US citizenship and clearance, listed here for completeness rather than as realistic targets for most international candidates.

### Tech companies with internal SOCs

Examples include Microsoft, Amazon AWS Security, Google Chronicle, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, and Splunk. These rarely hire Tier 1 externally, they more often hire experienced analysts or promote from within. Worth watching, not worth building an entire strategy around.

### Financial sector

Examples include JP Morgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, and HSBC. High demand and high standards, usually onsite in a financial hub.

### Other sectors worth searching broadly

Technology, telecommunications, healthcare, government, cloud providers, consulting, and critical infrastructure organisations all operate internal SOC functions with different experience requirements from MSSP positions. Search both paths rather than assuming one is universally easier.

## Company Research

Before applying, check what the company does, whether it operates a SOC or security service, the technologies mentioned in the vacancy, location requirements, working hours, required experience, required certifications, whether the role is genuinely remote, and whether the vacancy exists on the official careers site.

This reduces wasted applications.

These are US market figures and will be out of date eventually, treat them as a ceiling reference rather than an expectation. Check current, market specific figures for the actual role before relying on this table for a negotiation.

## Weekly Search Routine

A simple repeatable process is more useful than randomly checking job boards.

```text
MONDAY
Review new job alerts
Check priority company career pages
Save relevant roles

TUESDAY TO THURSDAY
Research saved roles
Tailor CV where appropriate
Submit applications
Record everything in tracker

FRIDAY
Review application pipeline
Follow up where appropriate
Identify repeated JD requirements
Update study priorities if a genuine skill gap appears

WEEKEND
Portfolio maintenance
Interview preparation
Hands on practice
Professional networking
```

The exact days are flexible. Consistency is the important part.

## Application Quality Check

Before submitting:

```text
[ ] I meet enough of the core requirements to justify applying
[ ] The role's location requirements work for me
[ ] My CV reflects relevant genuine skills
[ ] I have not claimed tools I have never used
[ ] Relevant portfolio projects are easy to find
[ ] The company and role have been researched
[ ] The vacancy is still active
[ ] The application is recorded in my tracker
```

---

## Final Principle

A job search should operate like an investigation.

Start broad. Filter irrelevant results. Prioritize stronger leads. Validate the information. Document what you did. Follow the evidence. Then improve the process based on what actually produces results.
